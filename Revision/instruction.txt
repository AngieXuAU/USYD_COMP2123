The exam will contain three questions for a total of 30 marks.

Question 1: True or False, 10 marks        (roughly 10 minutes)

There will be 10 True or False questions, worth 1 mark each. You have to shade a box. If you made a mistake, then you can shade the correct option and indicate which one to consider by writing 'T' or 'F'.

These questions will test understanding of concepts from across Weeks 1 to 7. They may ask about data structures, algorithms, running times, or properties discussed in lectures and tutorials.

These questions are intended to test conceptual understanding rather than lengthy calculations.

Question 2: Short answer questions, 8 marks         (roughly 10-15 minutes)

This question will contain four short problems involving the data structures and algorithms covered in Weeks 1 to 7.

The style will be similar to exercises where a data structure or algorithm is given and the task is to work through a small example. For example, a question might ask for the state of a data structure after some operations, the result of running an algorithm, a small computation involving a structure or something similar.

Answers to these parts should generally be short. There will not be a need to write a full algorithm design and correctness proof for every part.

Question 3: Algorithm and data structure design, 12 marks         (roughly 20-30 minutes)

This is the main problem-solving question and will require a more substantial answer.

For preparation for this question, place particular emphasis on Weeks 3 to 7, together with the algorithm analysis and correctness techniques introduced earlier in the unit. This question will be similar to the ones you have seen in Assignment 1.

The question may require designing or modifying a data structure or algorithm to satisfy particular running time and space requirements.

A complete answer may need to include:

A clear description of the algorithm or data structure.

An explanation of how any additional information is maintained.

Running time and space analysis.

A correctness argument.
